TEAM REMINT — TWO-MINUTE PITCH FOR JUDGES

Present My Climate Footprint as a teaching tool for schools. Use three
voices if you have them; with two people, combine Voices 2 and 3. One
person operates the game while another speaks. Read only the quoted
narration. The bracketed lines are screen cues.

0:00–0:22 | MAKE IT PERSONAL
[Begin with the team on camera, then show theslide.pdf as the opening
continues.]

VOICE 1 — SAY:
"Hi, we're Team Remint. Think of a young person you care about. One day,
they may buy a home or lead a community. They'll make those decisions in a
changing climate. They deserve to understand the risks and know what they
can do."

0:22–0:45 | INTRODUCE THE CLASSROOM TOOL
[Show the game's title, then press Start. Briefly show the regions on the
map. Choose Urban, then buy the 1950s weatherboard bungalow in River Valley.]

VOICE 2 — SAY:
"We built My Climate Footprint, a game teachers can use to make climate
change personal. Students choose a home and try to protect it for ten
years. Here by the river, we'll prepare for floods. In the hills, we'd
face landslides."

0:45–1:07 | GIVE THE CLASS A DECISION
[The year's question opens after the house is bought. Hold briefly on the
choices, select an answer, show its feedback, then close the question.]

VOICE 3 — SAY:
"Each year brings a choice about transport, waste or buildings. Teachers
can ask students to vote and explain why. The game imagines everyone
choosing the same way. It shows how shared decisions can increase or
reduce the pollution warming our planet, changing disaster risk in the game."

1:07–1:32 | PUT THE CHOICE INTO ACTION
[Open a + marker and buy 'Seal doors'. Show the house with the upgrade,
then press 'Finish Upgrades'. Let the calendar and weather animation play.]

VOICE 2 — SAY:
"Now we have limited money to protect our house. We'll seal the doors
against flooding. Students have to think: what matters most, and what can
wait? Then the year passes, and they see what happens to the home they've
invested in."

1:32–1:47 | TURN THE RESULT INTO A LESSON
[Press 'See the year review'. Hold on the review's Cause, Effect and What
helped panels.]

VOICE 3 — SAY:
"The review explains what happened and what could help. A teacher can ask:
what would you change next time? Students can try again, learning from
mistakes before the stakes are real."

1:47–2:00 | CLOSE
[Return to the team slide or show all team members on camera.]

VOICE 1 — SAY:
"Our COP31 goal is climate resilient cities. Tomorrow's homeowners and
leaders are in classrooms today. Let's help them protect the places
they'll call home. Thank you."

RECORDING NOTES
- The narration is about 230 words. The timings are rehearsal targets;
  practise with the clicks to fit two minutes.
- Pause briefly after "a young person you care about" and "before the
  stakes are real." Let the final line land before saying "Thank you."
- The first-time guide appears after Start. Dismiss it while recording,
  then cut that moment from the final video.
- The year's question changes between games. Choose an answer that fits
  the narration; there is no need to name a particular question.
- The weather result is random. The review works whether a disaster hits
  or not. If a disaster hits, pause briefly on the damaged house.
- The game's prices and disaster chances are teaching values, not real
  forecasts for an individual home. Avoid presenting them as predictions.
